## What to Do After a Hit-and-Run Accident

A hit-and-run is a crash in which a driver leaves without providing required information or help. Your first priorities are safety, medical care, evidence, and a prompt police report. Do not chase the fleeing driver.

> [!WARNING] Call 911 if anyone may be injured, traffic is in danger, a vehicle is on fire, or the fleeing driver poses an immediate threat. Give dispatch the vehicle description and direction of travel.

## The First 10 Minutes

### 1. Stop and Stay Safe

Pull over as close to the scene as you safely can. Turn on hazard lights. If the vehicles block traffic and can move, follow local law and the dispatcher's instructions about moving them.

Do not pursue the other vehicle. A chase can cause another crash, put you at risk, and make it harder to preserve the original scene.

### 2. Check for Injuries

Check yourself, passengers, pedestrians, and people in other vehicles. Ask for emergency medical help when needed. Adrenaline can mask pain, so do not dismiss new dizziness, confusion, severe headache, weakness, breathing trouble, or increasing pain.

### 3. Record What You Remember

As soon as it is safe, write or record:

- Full or partial plate number
- Make, model, color, and body type
- Damage already visible on the fleeing vehicle
- Driver or passenger description
- Direction of travel
- Time and exact location
- How the impact happened

A partial plate plus the state, vehicle type, and color may still help police.

### 4. Call Police

Report the crash from the scene. Give facts, not guesses. Ask for the incident or report number and the officer's name. If officers do not respond, ask how to file an online or counter report.

### 5. Photograph the Scene

Take wide, medium, and close photos. Include:

- Final vehicle positions
- Every damaged area
- Debris and paint transfer
- Skid marks or fluid
- Traffic lights and signs
- Lane markings and road conditions
- Nearby cameras and businesses
- Visible injuries, when appropriate

Photograph before moving debris or cleaning the vehicle, unless leaving it creates a safety hazard.

## Find Witnesses and Video

Ask witnesses to stay for police if possible. Get names and contact information. Do not coach their account. Ask what they personally saw and whether they have photos or dashcam video.

Look for cameras on stores, homes, buses, parking garages, traffic systems, and other vehicles. Ask the owner to preserve video for the relevant time window. Many systems overwrite files quickly. Police may need to request the recording.

Save your own dashcam file at once. Some cameras continuously overwrite old footage. Keep the original file and a backup.

## Exchange Information With Other People Involved

If another driver or property owner remains at the scene, exchange:

- Name and contact details
- Driver's license information
- Plate and vehicle information
- Insurance company and policy number
- Photos of documents, with permission

Do not admit fault or accuse someone. Describe what happened accurately to police and insurers.

## Get Medical Care

Emergency care is appropriate for serious or worsening symptoms. For non-emergency symptoms, seek timely medical advice. Tell the provider when and how the crash occurred. Keep discharge instructions, bills, prescriptions, and work restrictions.

Do not delay needed care to wait for the fleeing driver to be found. Insurance deadlines and medical-payment options vary, so report the crash promptly.

## Report the Claim to Your Insurer

Notify your insurer even if you hope police will find the driver. Give it:

- Police report number
- Photos and video
- Witness contacts
- The fleeing vehicle description
- Towing and storage location
- Medical information relevant to the claim
- Details about other damaged property

Answer factual questions. It is acceptable to say that you do not know or that an investigation is ongoing. Review any recorded-statement request and keep notes of each call.

## Coverage That May Apply

Coverage depends on state law and your policy. The declarations page shows which coverages and limits you bought.

### Uninsured Motorist Coverage

Uninsured motorist bodily injury, often called UMBI, may cover injuries caused by an unidentified hit-and-run driver. Some policies or states require physical contact, independent evidence, a prompt police report, or notice within a specific period.

Uninsured motorist property damage, or UMPD, may cover vehicle damage in some places. It is not available for hit-and-run property damage under every policy or state rule.

### Collision Coverage

Collision coverage generally pays for damage to your vehicle after a crash, minus your deductible, even if the other driver is unknown. It does not depend on police finding the driver.

### Medical Payments or Personal Injury Protection

Medical payments coverage or personal injury protection may pay eligible medical costs regardless of fault. PIP can also cover certain lost income or services, depending on state law and the policy.

### Rental and Towing Coverage

Rental reimbursement and roadside or towing coverage are usually optional. Ask about daily limits, total limits, approved rental classes, and when coverage ends.

> [!TIP] Ask the adjuster to identify each applicable coverage, limit, deductible, reporting deadline, and exclusion in writing.

## What If You Only Have Liability Insurance?

Liability coverage pays for damage or injury you cause to others. It generally does not repair your own car or pay your injuries after an unknown driver hits you. You may still have separate uninsured motorist, PIP, or medical payments coverage. Check the declarations page rather than assuming you have no claim.

If police later identify the driver, you can seek payment through that driver's liability insurer or other legal process. Recovery can be difficult if the driver has no insurance or assets.

## If Police Find the Driver

Send the new information to your insurer. Police charges and an insurance claim are separate processes. The other driver's insurer will still investigate identity, coverage, and fault.

If your insurer already paid under collision or uninsured motorist coverage, it may pursue recovery through subrogation. Ask whether recovery could reimburse your deductible or other out-of-pocket costs.

Do not contact or confront the driver yourself. Preserve messages, video, and witness information for police and insurers.

## If the Driver Is Never Found

Many hit-and-run cases remain unsolved. Your claim can still proceed under coverage you purchased. Keep a claim file with:

- Police report and updates
- Photos and original video files
- Repair estimates and invoices
- Towing, storage, and rental receipts
- Medical records and bills
- Wage-loss records
- A contact log for police and insurers

Follow up with the investigating agency if you obtain new evidence. Ask businesses or witnesses to retain original video until the case and claim are resolved.

## Hit-and-Run Reporting Deadlines

There is no single national deadline. Police reporting rules, insurance notice terms, and legal filing limits vary by state and policy. Some uninsured motorist provisions require notice much sooner than a lawsuit deadline.

Prompt action is safest. Report the crash, preserve evidence, and notify the insurer as soon as practical. If an insurer says notice was late, ask for the exact policy language and a written coverage decision.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Chasing the fleeing driver
- Leaving before police approve it, unless emergency care requires it
- Failing to record a partial plate or direction of travel
- Waiting to seek camera footage
- Cleaning paint transfer before taking photos
- Delaying the police report or insurance notice
- Repairing or disposing of the car before inspection
- Posting accusations or sensitive claim details online
- Accepting an early payment before knowing the full damage or injury impact

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I do first after a hit-and-run?

Stop safely, check for injuries, call police, and record the fleeing vehicle details. Do not chase the driver. Photograph the scene and find witnesses or cameras before evidence disappears.

### Will my insurance pay if the driver is not found?

Collision coverage may pay for vehicle damage after your deductible. Uninsured motorist, PIP, or medical payments coverage may apply to injuries or other losses. The result depends on your policy and state rules.

### Do I need a police report for a hit-and-run claim?

A police report is often important and may be required for uninsured motorist coverage. If officers do not respond, use the agency's approved online or counter-report process and keep the report number.

### What if I only got part of the license plate?

Report it. A partial plate combined with the state, vehicle color, make, model, damage, location, and travel direction can help police narrow the search.

### Should I follow the hit-and-run driver?

No. Following can create another crash or confrontation. Stay at the scene, call 911 when appropriate, and give police the vehicle description and direction of travel.

### How long do I have to report a hit-and-run?

Deadlines differ by jurisdiction and policy. Some coverage terms require prompt notice. Report the crash to police and your insurer as soon as practical instead of waiting for the general legal deadline.
